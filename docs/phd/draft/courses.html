PhD courses

Courses and reading groups of the Statistics curriculum, first and second year, academic year 2026/27

Courses, instructors, teaching periods and workload of the Statistics curriculum of the PhD in Economics, Statistics and Data Science. First-year courses are compulsory and end with a final exam; second-year reading groups are optional and also end with a final assessment. All teaching is in English. Click on a course to see its contents.

The official website of the PhD programme remains the reference; detailed timetables are in the Google calendars for the first year and the second year.

Instructors without an affiliation are from the University of Milano-Bicocca. Rows in grey are potential courses from the Big Data & Analytics for Business curriculum, not yet confirmed for the Statistics curriculum.

First year (cycle XLII)

15 compulsory courses and modules · 248 hours · 29 CFU

Term I · lectures 26 Oct – 18 Dec 2026 · exams 7 – 14 Jan 2027

Mathematical Analysis IFrancesco Battistoni (Univ. Cattolica)Course27 Oct – 12 Nov · 16 h · 2 CFU

Sequences and series of functions: pointwise and uniform convergence, uniform Cauchy condition, preservation of continuity and of the integral. Metric spaces, induced topology, compactness, completeness and density; normed spaces and the space C[0,1]. Cardinality, σ-algebras and Borel σ-algebras, measurable functions with values in a metric space.

Syllabus (PDF)

Mathematical Analysis IICarlo Alberto De Bernardi (Univ. Cattolica)Course17 Nov – 10 Dec · 16 h · 2 CFU

Measurable functions and approximation by simple functions. Positive measures, completion, Lebesgue measure via the outer measure, Carathéodory's extension theorem. Lebesgue integral: monotone convergence, Fatou's lemma, dominated convergence, comparison with the Riemann integral. Absolute continuity, Radon–Nikodym theorem and Lebesgue decomposition. Exercises.

Syllabus (PDF, common to Mathematical Analysis I–III)

Mathematical Analysis IIIEnrico Miglierina (Univ. Cattolica)Course14 – 18 Dec · 8 h · 1 CFU

Product measure and integration on product spaces; Tonelli and Fubini theorems with counterexamples. Lp spaces, Hölder and Minkowski inequalities, completeness of Lp for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞; the example of ℓp.

Syllabus (PDF, common to Mathematical Analysis I–III)

Term II · lectures 18 Jan – 24 Mar 2027 · exams 8 – 15 Apr 2027

Probability IPier Giovanni BissiriCourseTerm II · 16 h · 2 CFU

Axioms of probability and probability spaces; extension of probability measures and the Lebesgue measure on (0,1). Independence, conditional probability, Borel–Cantelli lemmas, tail σ-field and Kolmogorov's 0–1 law. Random variables and their distributions, the Lebesgue integral and expectation; monotone and dominated convergence, Fatou's lemma.

Probability IIFederico CamerlenghiCourseTerm II · 16 h · 2 CFU

Conditional expectations and probabilities, Radon–Nikodym theorem. Modes of convergence of random variables, weak convergence, Slutsky's theorem. Characteristic functions, tightness, Helly and Prokhorov theorems, Lévy's inversion and continuity theorems. Central limit theorems (Lindeberg–Lévy, Lindeberg–Lyapunov), laws of large numbers, infinitely divisible distributions.

Stochastic ProcessesMario BerahaCourseTerm II · 24 h · 3 CFU

Markov chains: transition probabilities, transience and recurrence, stationary distributions. Discrete-time martingales: stopping times, optional sampling, inequalities and convergence theorems. Continuous-time processes and the Poisson process. Brownian motion: existence, quadratic variation, reflection principle. Stochastic calculus: Itô integral and Itô's formula, Lévy's characterisation, Gaussian processes, stochastic differential equations and the Feynman–Kac theorem.

Statistical Inference I and IITommaso Rigon, Samantha Leorato (Univ. Milano)Starts in Term II and ends in Term III. Single exam.CourseTerm II – III · 48 h · 6 CFU

Likelihood inference: point and interval estimation, exponential families, sufficiency and conditionality, likelihood asymptotics; quasi-, pseudo- and composite likelihood. Modern hypothesis testing, from classical significance tests to post-selection inference: multiple comparisons, familywise error rate and false discovery rate control, simultaneous control of false discovery proportions.

Term III · lectures 19 Apr – 2 Jul 2027 · exams 12 – 16 Jul 2027

Bayesian Statistics IBernardo NipotiCourseTerm III · 16 h · 2 CFU

Principles of Bayesian statistics: exchangeability and de Finetti's theorem, the Bayesian framework, conjugate priors, point estimation, tests and credible intervals, the univariate and multivariate normal models, introduction to hierarchical modelling.

Bayesian Statistics IIRiccardo CorradinCourseTerm III · 16 h · 2 CFU

Bayesian computation, theory and programming: Metropolis–Hastings and Gibbs sampling with Rcpp and RcppArmadillo; adaptive Metropolis, MALA and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo; data augmentation, missing data and the EM algorithm; Laplace approximation, variational Bayes and expectation propagation, with applications to probit and logit models.

Bayesian Statistics IIISirio Legramanti (Univ. Bergamo)CourseTerm III · 16 h · 2 CFU

Mixture models in Bayesian statistics: finite and infinite mixtures, latent allocation variables and the induced clustering, components versus clusters. Exchangeable partition probability functions, priors on the number of components and of clusters. Posterior functionals and their approximation via MCMC; marginal and conditional algorithms, the Chinese restaurant process and its generalisations.

Computational Statistics IIAlessia Pini (Univ. Cattolica)Course of the Economics curriculum, optional for Statistics students.OptionalTerm III · 12 h · 1 CFU

The jackknife for bias estimation and reduction. The bootstrap: empirical distribution and plug-in principle, standard errors, confidence intervals (percentile, bootstrap-t, BCa), coverage and consistency, hypothesis testing. Introduction to the EM algorithm, with Gaussian mixtures and model-based clustering as examples.

Natural Language ProcessingErik Cambria (Nanyang Technological University, Singapore)From the Big Data & Analytics for Business curriculum. Not confirmed.PotentialTerm III · 12 h · 1 CFU

Natural language understanding: evolution of NLP, text preprocessing and representation, text classification, word embeddings, applications.

Term IV · lectures 6 Sep – 22 Oct 2027

Statistical Modelling IFederico Castelletti (Univ. Cattolica)CourseTerm IV · 12 h · 1 CFU

Graphical models: undirected graphs and DAGs, Markov properties and Markov equivalence; Gaussian and discrete graphical models; graphical lasso and the PC algorithm; Bayesian graphical model selection with MCMC; causal inference with directed acyclic graphs.

Statistical Modelling IIDaniele Spinelli, Giorgia ZaccariaCourseTerm IV · 12 h · 1 CFU

Mixture models for clustering and classification: Gaussian mixtures and parsimonious models, the EM algorithm, degeneracies and spurious solutions, model selection; mixtures of t distributions and of factor analysers. Robust statistics for multivariate data (minimum covariance determinant, forward search) and robust classification and clustering.

Statistical Modelling IIILuca BrusaCourseTerm IV · 12 h · 1 CFU

Latent variable models: linear and generalised linear mixed models for longitudinal data, discrete latent variable models, hidden Markov models for longitudinal data.

Statistical Modelling IVFrancesco Bartolucci (Univ. Perugia)CourseTerm IV · 12 h · 1 CFU

Causal inference and impact evaluation: the potential outcomes framework, randomised trials, selection on observables and propensity score methods, difference-in-differences, causal inference with discrete latent variable models.

Explainable AI for Business ValueFabio MercorioFrom the Big Data & Analytics for Business curriculum. Not confirmed.PotentialTerm IV · 12 h · 1 CFU

Second year (cycle XLI)

6 optional reading groups · 12 hours and 1 CFU each

Term I · 26 Oct – 18 Dec 2026

Approximate Bayesian Computational MethodsAugusto Fasano (Univ. Torino and Collegio Carlo Alberto)Reading groupTerm I · 12 h · 1 CFU

Theoretical and computational aspects of the most widely used approximate methods for Bayesian inference when the posterior is intractable or MCMC is computationally impractical, with a focus on binary and categorical regression. Four modules:

  • Laplace approximation, Variational Bayes and Expectation Propagation: general principles and theoretical guarantees.
  • Variational Bayes for binary regression: logit and probit models.
  • Expectation Propagation in practice: the clutter problem and probit regression.
  • Extensions to categorical data, dynamic settings and beyond.

Syllabus with references (PDF)

Probabilistic Preference LearningValeria Vitelli (Univ. Oslo)Reading groupTerm I · 12 h · 1 CFU

Aims of a preference learning analysis and types of preference data. Probabilistic models for rankings: distance-based models on the space of permutations and score-based models. Maximum likelihood and Bayesian inference, clustering of preference data with mixture models. Practical sessions in R; no prior experience with ranking models is required.

Term II · 18 Jan – 24 Mar 2027

Automated Machine Learning and Neural Architecture SearchAntonio CandelieriReading groupTerm II · 12 h · 1 CFU

Automated machine learning: algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimisation with a limited number of trials; neural architecture search for deep networks. Basics of sequential model-based optimisation (Bayesian optimisation), the standard methodology for AutoML and NAS.

Some Issues on Statistical ModellingRiccardo Borgoni, Paolo MaranzanoReading groupTerm II · 12 h · 1 CFU

Missing data: mechanisms, weighting and imputation. Measurement error and its effect on regression models. Quantile regression: fundamentals and inference. Implementations and case studies in R.

Spatio-temporal DataPaolo Maranzano, Riccardo BorgoniReading groupTerm II · 12 h · 1 CFU

Models and techniques for data with spatial and temporal dependence: detecting dependence (autocorrelation functions, spatial correlograms); resampling and validation schemes for temporal data (block and moving-window resampling, hv-blocking, prequential approach, temporal bootstrap) and for spatio-temporal data (leave-location-out, leave-time-out, nearest neighbour distance matching, spatio-temporal partitioning); R packages.
